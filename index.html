Research and publishing in games, narrative, and media arts

IGWT Institute for Games, Written-narrative & Tropes is a Canadian not-for-profit research institute and publisher based in Toronto. The institute conducts critical and scholarly study of games as an art form, visual novels, interactive narrative, and animation, with particular attention to East Asian popular media.

Mission

IGWT Institute examines the formal, narrative, and aesthetic structures of digital games, visual novels, animation, and related media arts. Our work focuses on close textual and ludic analysis, narrative theory, and the study of tropes across interactive and animated media, especially works from East Asia.

As an independent academic and critical publisher, the institute is preparing two serial publications: Ludotextual Studies, a double-anonymous peer-reviewed Diamond Open Access journal, and IGWT, a semi-annual magazine of criticism, interviews, and close readings. Through open-access scholarship and long-form criticism, the institute supports rigorous inquiry into contemporary narrative media.

Areas of study

Games as an art form

Critical and scholarly study of game design, mechanics, and expressive form across independent and commercial titles.

Visual novels and written narrative

Close analysis of scripting, branching structures, character conventions, and genre traditions in visual novels and text-driven games.

Interactive narrative and tropes

Investigation of player agency, controlling ideas, metanarrative devices, and recurring narrative tropes in interactive media.

Animation and East Asian media

Research on animation, anime-style games, and related popular media arts across East Asian cultural contexts.

Narrative Momentum and Villain Characterization in the Far Cry Series

July 2025

Excerpt · July 2025

In game narrative, the antagonist often serves as the primary engine driving the plot forward, and the success of this characterization directly shapes the tension of the story and the depth of player engagement. The more intricate the villain's scheme and the greater the threat they pose, the more compelling the dramatic conflict becomes, forcing the protagonist to grow more resourceful and inventive. In Ubisoft's Far Cry series in particular, villain characterization has become a defining hallmark: the centre of nearly every instalment's cover art is occupied by its principal antagonist, from the flamboyant and elegant Pagan Min in Far Cry 4 and the fanatical, brooding Joseph Seed in Far Cry 5 to the ruthless and complex Anton Castillo in Far Cry 6. A well-constructed antagonist can sustain an entire narrative framework and leave a lasting impression on the player.
